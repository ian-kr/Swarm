Mission briefing

Hold the base against a swarm that learns.

Six attack drones are inbound from the north. You have a budget of 15 to build your air defense:

  • Radar detects drones, but hills block its line of sight.
  • Jammer scrambles drone navigation nearby.
  • Interceptor pad launches 3 interceptors at any detected drone.

The enemy swarm was trained with reinforcement learning on AMD GPUs, playing millions of battles against random defenses until it learned to find the gaps. Your job is to leave it none.
Under the hood

How the red team learned to attack

Training curve
Pipeline
  1. Terrain tables: precomputed line-of-sight between every pair of cells and all-pairs Dijkstra shortest paths. Pathfinding and radar checks become O(1) tensor lookups.
  2. Batched simulation: thousands of battles stepped together as PyTorch tensor ops on the GPU (ROCm on AMD).
  3. Threat field: for every battle, a GPU Bellman-Ford pass computes a threat-aware distance to the base (the A* cost model) for every cell. Each drone sees this pathfinding field.
  4. PPO self-improvement: one shared policy network flies every drone. Rewards for progress and breaching, penalties for being shot down or running out of time. Each drone can also choose follow the pathfinder, so it learns when to trust A*-style routing and when to improvise.
  5. Arms race: a genetic search keeps finding defenses the current swarm can barely beat, and the swarm trains against them.
  6. Difficulty: Veteran is the best checkpoint on strong defenses. Regular and Recruit fly the same network with noisier decisions.
  7. Interceptors chase tracked drones along Dijkstra next-hop tables. The Scripted enemy uses threat-aware A*.
