      Technical annex

      How the red team learned to attack

      Training curve

      Pipeline

      1. Terrain tables. Line of sight between every pair of cells and all-pairs Dijkstra shortest paths are precomputed, so radar checks and pathfinding become lookups.
      2. Batched simulation. Thousands of battles step together as PyTorch tensor operations on the GPU (ROCm on AMD).
      3. Threat field. For every battle, a GPU Bellman-Ford pass computes a threat-aware distance to the base for every cell: the A* cost model. Each drone sees this field.
      4. PPO. One shared policy network flies every drone. Rewards for progress and breaching, penalties for being shot down and a larger one for loitering until time runs out. Each drone can also choose follow the pathfinder, so it learns when to trust A*-style routing and when to improvise.
      5. Arms race. A genetic search keeps finding defenses the current swarm can barely beat, or refuses to attack, and the swarm trains against them.
      6. Difficulty. Veteran is the best checkpoint on strong defenses. Regular and Recruit fly the same network with noisier decisions.
      7. Interceptors chase tracked drones along Dijkstra next-hop tables. The Scripted enemy uses threat-aware A*.
